Manifesto & Mission

Manifesto — Build the commons together

Europe and China run on the same open foundations: kernels, cryptographic libraries, compilers, open instruction sets and open standards. Both now treat open source as strategic, and both invest in it, though in different ways. Neither can secure on its own the code that both depend on.

We believe:

  1. Digital commons are infrastructure, not a battlefield. Openly licensed code is a public good. Its value grows with every user and every contributor, whatever their nationality.
  2. Compete above, cooperate below. Products and markets are for competition. The shared layer underneath is best maintained together.
  3. Sovereignty means being able to understand, maintain, adapt and fork the code. It does not mean isolation. Open source is the only form of digital sovereignty that needs no walls, and it grows stronger through cooperation.
  4. Pay once, reuse everywhere. Software that one public administration pays for should be usable by others, at home and abroad.
  5. Science grows in the open. Shared research software, data and models are natural bridges.
  6. Trust comes from evidence. That means transparent funding and governance, security screening, and respect for the law on both sides.
  7. Neutral ground, small steps. We work through foundations and standards bodies with open governance, and take concrete steps that can be measured and scaled.

If you think this should happen and you share these assumptions, you are already one of us.

Mission

Open research and open engagement for EU–China cooperation on the digital commons.

The Observatory turns the manifesto into work along two lines that reinforce each other:

  1. Open research. We map how Europe and China make policy for open source and digital commons, and how they fund, govern and reuse them. We cover every level, from the EU and the central government to cities, foundations and communities. Every source is archived, every claim is labelled, and every dataset is open and can be corrected. The research is independent: it informs cooperation and does not speak for any government or company.
  2. Stakeholder engagement. We bring the evidence to everyone who shapes these policies: public administrations, research funders, foundations and communities, companies, universities and diplomats. The aim is that they see the potential and the concrete opportunities of cooperating on the commons, and that they join the research itself: contributing cases and data, correcting our findings, and setting the questions.

Goal: an evidence-based agenda for non-competitive EU–China cooperation on the digital commons, built and owned by its stakeholders.
