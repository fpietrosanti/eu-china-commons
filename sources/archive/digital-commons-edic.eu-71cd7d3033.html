Mission

DC-EDIC develops, maintains, and scales digital commons for Europe, pooling resources across member states to reduce technological dependency.

Digital commons for Europe

Digital commons include open-source software, open standards and shared digital infrastructure. Governments, communities, companies and individuals develop them collectively, govern them openly and adapt them freely. They provide an alternative to technological dependency.

Maintaining the full digital commons ecosystem requires resources from several countries. The Digital Commons EDIC pools those resources to keep the components Europe depends on professionally maintained, interoperable and available to all.

What DC-EDIC does

Our mandate is structured around five actions, co-funded by the European Union through the Digital Europe Programme.

Build a European community

We work alongside open-source communities across member states, organise tracks at FOSDEM and other events, and maintain relationships with component maintainers. The goal is to connect deployment experience with development priorities.

Open access to funding

We build an expertise hub and one-stop-shop so public administrations and enterprises can find their way through procurement, access migration support, and connect with qualified integrators across borders.

Sustain the components everyone depends on

All modern software depends on open-source infrastructure components, yet their maintenance is chronically underfinanced. Many are maintained by individuals around the world working without institutional support. This underfunding weakens the resilience of the software that relies on them. We are piloting a European Sovereign Tech Fund with Germany’s Sovereign Tech Agency to coordinate European investment in critical open digital infrastructure, working with the global community that maintains it.

Bring deployment evidence to policymakers

We share deployment data and operational experience with European policymakers. When the debate turns to procurement directives or Cyber Resilience Act implementation, we contribute what we observe across our national deployments.

Run shared projects

We run 100-Day Challenges that produce working open-source software where a critical component or integration is missing. The teams work to a defined scope, develop the software in the open and test it with users. We also coordinate with existing communities and foundations to support their work. See our first projects.

How we work

Used in everyday work. We measure success by whether public servants, developers and businesses use the software in their daily work and whether it makes that work easier.

Maintained for the long run. We fund maintainers, secure dependencies, and invest in the work that keeps digital commons reliable. Existing infrastructure that works well deserves sustained investment.

Connected across borders. We link tools developed in different countries so that what works in one can serve another. A tool built in France can be adapted in Germany, tested in The Netherlands, deployed in Italy.

Open by default. All software jointly developed within DC-EDIC is released under licences that qualify as both Free Software (Free Software Foundation) and Open Source (Open Source Initiative).

What are digital commons?

Digital commons are digital resources defined by distributed and communal production, ownership, and governance. Their governance includes access and sharing rules designed to sustain both the resource and the community around it, and to prevent any single actor from capturing exclusive control.

Digital commons support commercial use, and many successful businesses are built on and around them. Their shared governance prevents enclosure: turning the shared resource into a proprietary one.

Examples include open-source software, open data, open standards and shared digital infrastructure that anyone can adapt and whose development and governance are shared.
