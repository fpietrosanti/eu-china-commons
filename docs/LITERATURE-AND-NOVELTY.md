## 2. What appears to be new in our work

What follows is a hypothesis to confirm with a systematic search.

1. **First comparison against the adopted DC-EDIC Implementation Strategy.** It was adopted on 16 Oct 2026, so no external analysis can yet have mapped Chinese instruments onto its eight results (E1–E8), the Annex C criteria and its entry points.
2. **A complementarity lens, not only a threat lens.** Western literature is dominated by decoupling and security. We found no work that scores fit, feasibility and exposure separately.
3. **Instrument-level reading of the 2025–26 regional policies:** Beijing 2026–28, Shanghai Dec 2025, Zhejiang/Hangzhou, Jiangsu Zijin, Guangdong/Shenzhen, Chongqing. The most recent European description (OFE 2023) predates all of them.
4. **Comparison of funding models.** The EU funds maintenance of what exists (EU-STF/STA); China funds incubation and adoption. We found no prior work that sets them side by side or names additionality as the comparison metric.
5. **Eclipse–OpenAtom treated as a governance case study.** In the literature so far it appears only as a security concern (House letter, 2025).
6. **Evidence discipline.** We keep an archived, hashed source register and separate fact, commitment, inference and proposal. Western sources often omit this distinction, for example by treating fund envelopes as spending.

## 3. Consequences for the paper and website

- **We must engage the security literature directly.** The House Select Committee letter of June 2025 targets the very precedent we use (Eclipse–OpenAtom). The paper needs a section answering it: audits, fork capability, separation of governance. Otherwise reviewers and policy-makers will dismiss the case study.
- **Positioning statement (draft):** "The first independent assessment of China's open-source and digital-commons policies against the EU DC-EDIC Implementation Strategy, separating complementarity from risk."
- The OFE 2023 report is our natural "previous state of the art". Cite it and show what changed: new Chinese plans, plus the EU Open Source Strategy (June 2026) and the DC-EDIC.
